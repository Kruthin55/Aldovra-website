Systems Product Industrialization Engineer

Philips · Pune

About the role

Job Title Systems Product Industrialization Engineer Job Description Systems Product Industrialization Engineer As an Ultrasound Systems Product Industrialization Engineer, you will help turn new R&D designs into products that can be manufactured safely, consistently, efficiently, and at scale. You will work directly with R&D, Manufacturing, Quality, Test Engineering, and suppliers to bring new Ultrasound Systems into production, implement design and process changes, and remotely help to solve technical problems encountered on the manufacturing floor. Your Role: Partner with R&D and Manufacturing to transition new and modified Ultrasound System designs into production. Evaluate new designs for manufacturability, assembly, testability, process capability, and production risk using DFM/DFA/DfX principles. Develop and improve manufacturing processes, tooling, equipment, assembly methods, and production documentation. Plan and execute engineering builds, manufacturing-readiness activities, process validations, Installation Qualifications, and Equipment Qualifications. Create and maintain Process FMEAs, manufacturing control plans, process requirements, assembly instructions, validation documentation, and other controlled manufacturing records. Create and release Manufacturing Bills of Materials, routings, and associated manufacturing master data. Remotely troubleshoot technical issues on production lines and help restore production quickly when manufacturing problems result in line-down conditions. Perform structured root cause analysis for design, manufacturing, process, quality, yield, and nonconforming-product issues. Implement approved product and process changes across manufacturing locations and applicable supplier sites. Apply Lean Manufacturing and continuous-improvement methods to reduce waste, simplify processes, improve cycle time, increase yield, and lower manufacturing cost. Support engineering builds and testing needed to evaluate new products, design changes, and manufacturing processes. Identify and communicate manufacturing risks to R&D and project teams, including challenging designs or schedules when product quality, manufacturability, safety, or compliance could be compromised. Ensure manufacturing processes and documentation comply with applicable quality, regulatory, safety, environmental, and company requirements. Support geographically distributed engineering and manufacturing teams, which will require early-morning or evening collaboration. You're the Right Fit If: Bachelor's Degree / Vocational Education in Mechanical Engineering, Electronics Engineering, Sciences, Cost Engineering, Manufacturing Engineering, Lean Manufacturing, Industrial Automation or equivalent. Experience or academic exposure to manufacturing engineering, Lean Manufacturing, Process FMEA, process validation, root cause analysis, DFM/DFA/DfX, engineering change control, manufacturing documentation, or cost analysis is beneficial. For an entry-level candidate, demonstrated technical problem-solving ability and the capacity to learn are more important than having experience in every area.

Qualifications

We are looking for an early-career engineer who combines solid engineering fundamentals curiosity, initiative, and a willingness to learn. The successful candidate will: Apply analytical thinking and engineering fundamentals to solve well-defined manufacturing problems. Take ownership of assigned problems and drive them through resolution rather than waiting for step-by-step direction. Know when to work independently and when an issue should be escalated or additional expertise is needed. Be comfortable troubleshooting problems directly in a manufacturing environment. Learn unfamiliar products, processes, and technologies quickly. Produce clear, accurate, and professional engineering documentation. Communicate technical issues effectively with both engineering and manufacturing audiences. Work effectively across different functions, personalities, cultures, locations, and time zones. Manage multiple projects and production priorities while responding appropriately to urgent manufacturing needs. Maintain a strong commitment to product quality and be willing to raise concerns when schedule, cost, or other pressures could compromise quality or compliance. .
